01 The problem

The decision: where do today’s tailoring hours go?

About 20 new postings arrived every weekday (up to 87 on a Monday), many of them reposts. Triaging them by hand cost about an hour a day, all to answer one question: which two or three roles get a tailored application today?

What I owned

  • The rubric and scoring rules
  • The pipeline architecture and sign-off gates
  • The brief’s design
  • Diagnosing every failure

What stays human

  • Which roles to apply to
  • Approving each step before it’s written
  • Every application I submit
  • Changing the criteria
02 Architecture

Three scheduled tasks, one source of truth

Each stage does one job. Nothing downstream edits what’s upstream: the digest and brief are rebuilt from the tracker every time.

03 Measurement

Making “fit” explicit

My criteria are a written rubric. Must-haves and red flags reject a role outright; everything else starts from a base-fit judgment and moves by named amounts.

Must-haves

Fail one and the role is rejected
  • New York area, or remote
  • A pay floor (top of band)

Red flags

Automatic reject
  • ML engineering dressed as “data science”
  • Posting closed to applicants

Boosts

Raise the score
  • A 0–3 year experience bar
  • Fresh posting, few applicants

Penalties

Lower the score
  • Reposted or stale (heavy)
  • 5+ years, or a Staff/Lead title

05 Debugging

The hardest bugs returned confident wrong answers

In week one, 42% of postings came back without a description, yet the scores still looked complete. I listed the possible explanations and tested each one.

✗

Window too small?

Already full-size; resizing changed nothing.

✗

Background tab paused the page?

Forced it to “visible” — still nothing.

✗

Rate limiting?

The same postings loaded instantly, back to back, from another source.

✓

A background request the page depends on failed every time

Confirmed in network traffic. A server-rendered source returned all 22 test postings.

All 42 early failures were later re-fetched. Failed rows are flagged and retried, so a bad morning never leaves a permanent gap.
06 Iteration

Its own logs changed the design

The tracker doubles as usage data. It showed that most of Monday’s pile was reposts.

Share of new listings that were reposts

Jul 22 – Oct 21. Hover or tap a bar for counts.

The change: Monday triage mode. Reposts were 61% of listings on Mondays and Tuesdays, against 23% later in the week. Now, after a weekend, every listing gets the cheap checks but only the 25 most promising get a full read; the rest are logged, not lost.
Next

Validating the judge

Next I’ll test how good its judgments are:

  • Agreement: do its scores match my own, rated blind?
  • Reliability: does it give the same posting the same score twice?
  • Calibration: does a 7 mean the same thing for every kind of role?
  • Pre-screen recall: how many good roles does the title-only filter throw away?
